The Run, Folded Twice

The locked record made the machinery a ledger you can read without opening. This proposal changes what the ledger looks like before you open it. Everything the agent did between two things it said becomes one line. That line opens to the ledger; each ledger line opens to its detail. Two folds, three states — and nothing new drawn, only a boundary widened and a threshold moved.

What to judge it by: §03, the same reply as shipped and as proposed, with the room's activity act live on it; §06, the same rule measured against twenty-two real sessions on the running daemon; and §07, five questions drawn as options. Everything else is the reasoning.

§02

The claim, and five rules

A run is everything the agent did between two things it said. Closed, it is one sentence. Open, it is the ledger. Each line opens to its detail.

The record's five laws stand (T-1 … T-5). These five sit under them and are numbered R because they are the rules of the run.

R-1

Speech is the boundary. A prompt, a reply paragraph, a team message: these open and close runs. Reasoning, tool calls, notices and summaries are what happened between them and belong to the run. Today a run is a contiguous stretch of tool rows and any thought or notice cuts it in two; under R-1 the same thought sits inside.

R-2

Two lines make a run; one line is the line. A run of a single line — one read, one command, one thought, one marker — draws as that line, exactly as today. No head over one thing. This is what keeps the rule harmless for an agent that says a sentence between every call (§06, antigravity).

R-3

The head is the record's closing sentence, moved to the front. Nouns, never verbs: acts first, then looks, each with its count; the changed files named up to four; then, at the right where a narrow column cannot hide it, the failure count in red beside the span; the run's first stamp in the margin. It is the same sentence §03 D already draws past eight lines — it now draws at two.

R-4

Opening is downward only. The head opens to the ladder (the first fold). A ladder line opens to its detail, a diff, a diagnostic, a child run (the second fold). The room's activity act opens every head at once and never any line; with a head on every run, the act keeps the head as the ladder's title rather than hiding it.

R-5

Hue, time and landing keep their grammar. Red for what did not complete; the green breath for what is still happening — and a live run's head carries the line that is happening. Every row keeps its anchor and its timeline_id; a search hit or a task launcher landing on a row inside a closed run opens the run and the line, as a landing inside an eight-line fold already does.

Build order — clients/app

  1. lib/transcript/turns.ts — buildSegments stops flushing the run on thinking, notice and summary rows and takes the sitting cut points so a run never straddles a pill. The ledger segment gains ordered members: lines, a thought, a marker, a summary. Team collaboration rows keep closing runs.
  2. lib/transcript/ledger.ts — runSummary orders nouns acts first, carries the changed files up to FOLD_NAME_LIMIT, counts runtime-error notices into failed, and sets closedByDefault at two lines with no task exception. RUN_FOLD_THRESHOLD becomes 1.
  3. components/transcript/Ledger.tsx — the head draws the names, the live state (dot, current line, open span) and the error count; the ladder renders members by kind through the existing Thinking, Marker and summary drawings. focusInside already opens a run for a landing.
  4. components/transcript/Thinking.tsx — the line form for a thought that stands alone, opening to the clamp; the clamp form unchanged inside a ladder.
  5. styles/transcript.css — .tr-fold .names, .tr-fold .cur, the ladder's member margins, and the act keeping the head ([data-fold="off"]). No token.
  6. Tests — test/transcript-ledger.test.mjs and transcript-turns.test.mjs: run boundaries across thinking and notices, the sitting cut, acts-first order and names, the two-line default, a thought-only run, an error inside a run. What it costs. Three view-model modules and one component, a day and a half with the tests. document-model.ts's representatives, the head of the document, the day pill and the tail controller are untouched: every row keeps its timeline_id and its element, and a closed run's rows are hidden by the same CSS the eight-line fold hides them with today.

What it cannot fix. The two the record already named: no started_at on openers, so a head's span is stamps not durations; and Codex's opaque exec envelope, whose lines read Ran commands with nothing to open — the head at least says 3 tool batches. Both are adapter work in Rust, not this view's.
